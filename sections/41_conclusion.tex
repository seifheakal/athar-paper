This paper revisited the ViT-Base/16 pipeline for vehicle re-identification on VeRi-776. We separated the effect of the training recipe from the effect of evaluation-time post-processing. Our recipe combines CLIP pretraining initialisation, a tuned metric-learning objective, an optimised augmentation pipeline, AdamW optimisation and a three-step post-processing pipeline. It reaches 89.97\% mAP and 97.79\% Rank-1 at its best-mAP operating point. A separate best-Rank-1 operating point reaches 85.14\% mAP and 98.33\% Rank-1. This number comes from the post-processing pipeline, not from the recipe. At the raw-embedding level the recipe only matches TransReID (82.2\% vs.\ 82.3\%). Given our identical post-processing, the official TransReID and CLIP-ReID checkpoints reach \textbf{91.12\%} and \textbf{91.80\%} mAP, both above ours (Section~\ref{sec:matched}). So we present Stream~1 as a reproducible, specific baseline, not as a superior recipe. We see this as the most useful finding of the paper for the community. It suggests that a large part of the apparent progress after TransReID on this benchmark comes from post-processing, not from architecture or recipe changes. Post-processing needs no retraining, AQE costs almost nothing, and re-ranking is practical with our sparse implementation (Section~\ref{sec:cost}).

Score-level fusion with a CLIP-SENet branch~\cite{lu2025clipsenet} reaches 93.32\% mAP and 98.45\% Rank-1. This is slightly the best published mAP on VeRi-776, above the previous best (CLIP-SENet standalone, 92.9\%~\cite{lu2025clipsenet}) by a narrow $+0.42$\,pp. The Rank-1 (98.45\%) is competitive with the CLIP-SENet standalone Rank-1 (98.7\%), but does not exceed it. A paired bootstrap confirms that the gain over the stronger single stream is statistically significant. So the fusion shows real complementarity between the transformer and CNN-semantic streams. The full ablation from 82.3\% to 93.3\% mAP traces this trajectory under one consistent protocol. The zero-shot VeRi-Wild study shows which parts transfer. The ViT-B embedding generalises in part, but the fusion advantage and the re-ranking gain are domain-specific.

Future directions include knowledge distillation from the fusion model into a single-stream model, and learned query-adaptive fusion toward the measured oracle headroom (Section~\ref{sec:veriwild}). They also include evaluation under seen/unseen vehicle-type protocols instead of standard random-identity splits, and extension to nighttime surveillance and CityFlowV2. All code and checkpoints are publicly released.
