\subsection{Re-ranking and post-processing}
Zhong et al.~\cite{zhong2017rerank} (CVPR 2017) introduced $k$-reciprocal encoding. The idea is simple. If a gallery image is among the $k$-reciprocal nearest neighbours of a probe, it is more likely to be a true match. A Jaccard-distance-based score is blended with the original cosine distance. This improves precision and needs no labelled data. Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2022vehiclererank} adapted this idea to vehicle ReID with orientation-guided query expansion. They show that choosing neighbours with a consistent viewpoint helps queries that are very sensitive to viewpoint. Moral et al.~\cite{moral2023syntheticrerank} also studied vehicle-specific post-processing. They use gallery video-clip information in the re-ranking step on CityFlow-ReID and VeRi-776.

Average Query Expansion (AQE)~\cite{arandjelovic2012aqe} replaces each query feature with the mean of its top-$k$ retrieved gallery features. This smooths the query space and improves mAP at almost no extra cost. Database-side Augmentation (DBA) does the same for gallery features and can be combined with AQE. We evaluate AQE alone, because it gives the best mAP--Rank-1 trade-off at $k{=}3$ for our ViT-B stream.

Supervised Contrastive Learning (SupCon)~\cite{khosla2020supcon} extends contrastive losses to the supervised setting. All samples of the same class in a mini-batch count as positives. This gives embedding spaces that are more uniform and more discriminative than triplet loss alone~\cite{ye2022survey}. We evaluate SupCon as an alternative metric objective for Stream~1 (variant A5$\beta$). The deployed checkpoint (A5$\alpha$) keeps a triplet $+$ centre objective. Section~\ref{sec:seed_variance} shows that the two are statistically equivalent.
