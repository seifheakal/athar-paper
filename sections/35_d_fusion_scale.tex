\subsection{Why score-level fusion works}
The complementarity comes from the architecture. The ViT-B backbone processes images as global patch-token sequences with multi-head self-attention. This gives representations that are sensitive to the holistic structure of the vehicle and to long-range spatial relationships~\cite{he2021transreid,dosovitskiy2021vit}. The CLIP-SENet ResNet101-IBN-a backbone~\cite{pan2018ibn} applies hierarchical local convolutional filters that capture fine-grained texture. IBN gives style normalisation and discriminative training at the same time. The AFEM branch adds attribute-level structure from TinyCLIP~\cite{lu2025clipsenet}, which is orthogonal to ViT patch attention. Score-level fusion keeps the post-processing calibration of each stream independent, and it needs no additional training. The optimal $w_2{=}0.65$ for CLIP-SENet reflects its higher standalone post-processed performance (91.44\% vs.\ 89.97\% mAP for ViT-B). The ViT-B branch still adds $+1.88$\,pp above CLIP-SENet alone. This confirms genuine complementarity.

\subsection{Backbone scale and overfitting}
We trained ViT-L/14 (304\,M parameters) under the same recipe as a higher-capacity alternative. Despite its larger size, it overfit the 576 training identities of VeRi-776, which is a known failure mode at this dataset scale. It did not exceed the ViT-B/16 CLIP result after re-ranking. This supports putting systematic post-processing ahead of backbone scaling as the main performance lever for small-scale ReID benchmarks.
