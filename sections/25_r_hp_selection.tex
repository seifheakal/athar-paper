\subsection{Hyperparameter selection, held-out validation, and significance}
\label{sec:holdout}
We report all post-processing hyperparameters---AQE $k$, the $k$-reciprocal
re-ranking triple $(k_1,k_2,\lambda)$, and the fusion weight $w_1$---as
selected by grid search on the VeRi-776 query/gallery split, and we treat this
as a source of potential optimistic bias rather than leaving it implicit. To
state the protocol once and unambiguously: \emph{every headline number in this
paper uses these split-tuned values}; the validation analyses below quantify
and bound the optimism of that choice---they are audits of the headline
configuration, not its source.

Three observations bound that bias. First, the selection surface is flat, not
peaked: fused mAP stays above $93.0\%$ across the entire range
$w_1\in[0.2,0.5]$ (Figure~\ref{fig:weight}), so the reported result does not
sit on a sharp test-set optimum. Second, the re-ranking and AQE settings are
close to established defaults ($k_1{=}80,k_2{=}15,\lambda{=}0.2$ lie within the
ranges recommended by Zhong et al.~\cite{zhong2017rerank}; AQE $k{=}3$ is a
standard small neighbourhood), so they are not idiosyncratically fit to
VeRi-776. Third, the test set (200 identities, 1\,678 queries) is large
relative to the small number of scalar hyperparameters selected, limiting the
degrees of freedom available for overfitting.
