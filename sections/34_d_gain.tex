\subsection{What drives the ViT-B gain}
The improvement of our ViT-B pipeline over the published TransReID baseline combines recipe effects and post-processing effects, and the ablation deliberately separates them. Within the recipe, CLIP pretraining initialisation and AdamW with layer-wise LR decay are the two ingredients that our development depended on most (A2, A4). CLIP provides a rich visual-semantic prior for fine-grained vehicle discrimination, which fits transfer-learning observations in the ReID literature~\cite{li2023clipreid,radford2021clip}. We repeat the caveat of Section~\ref{sec:matched}. At matched no-post-processing conditions the recipe does not by itself exceed TransReID (82.2\% vs.\ 82.3\% mAP). Under our identical post-processing, both TransReID and CLIP-ReID are above our pipeline (91.12\% and 91.80\% vs.\ 89.97\%). So these are drivers within our recipe, not net gains over a tuned baseline. Most of the reported improvement comes from AQE, $k$-reciprocal re-ranking and TTA. For context, we also tried a DINOv2-initialised alternative~\cite{oquab2023dinov2} as a second fusion stream on VeRi-776. It did not match the CLIP-based CLIP-SENet stream (below 89.3\% mAP standalone), so we kept CLIP-family semantic supervision for both streams. We did not run a controlled DINOv2-versus-CLIP Stream-1 backbone swap on VeRi-776. So we make no claim about pretraining scale versus semantic supervision from this benchmark alone.
