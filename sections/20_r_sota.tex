Read this table with care, because the rows mix post-processing conditions. First, our ViT-B pipeline (89.97\% mAP, \cmark) is the strongest pure-ViT-B entry among published results. But those baselines are reported without post-processing (\xmark). When we apply our identical post-processing to the official TransReID and CLIP-ReID checkpoints, they reach 91.12\% and 91.80\% mAP, both above ours (Section~\ref{sec:matched}). So this row reflects post-processing, not a recipe advantage. Among single models reported with post-processing, MBR4B-LAI (92.1\% mAP~\cite{luo2023mbr4b}) is also above our single stream. Second, the fusion (93.32\% mAP) is slightly above the best prior published result (CLIP-SENet, 92.9\%~\cite{lu2025clipsenet}), by $+0.42$\,pp, and above MBR4B-LAI by $+1.23$\,pp. We read this as evidence that the two streams complement each other, not as a decisive margin.
