\subsection{Matched-condition comparison with TransReID and CLIP-ReID}
\label{sec:matched}
The comparisons above apply post-processing to our pipeline but quote prior
ViT-B baselines at their published, post-processing-free operating points. To
close this gap---the central question of whether our recipe or our
post-processing is responsible for the reported numbers---we ran the official
TransReID VeRi-776 checkpoint (\texttt{TransReID*} ViT-B/16, released by the
TransReID authors~\cite{he2021transreid}) and the official CLIP-ReID
ViT-B/16 VeRi-776 checkpoint~\cite{li2023clipreid} through our identical
post-processing (AQE $k{=}3$; $k$-reciprocal re-ranking
$k_1{=}80,k_2{=}15,\lambda{=}0.2$), using each repository's own released
dataloader and inference path. Table~\ref{tab:matched} and
Figure~\ref{fig:matched} report the results.

The finding is unambiguous and, for our own claims, cautionary: once all three
models receive the same post-processing, the original TransReID reaches
91.12\% mAP and the original CLIP-ReID 91.80\%---both above our
89.97\%---while at the raw-embedding level all three are within 0.9\,pp
(82.11\,/\,82.99\,/\,82.22\%). The TransReID base value reproduces its
published number ($\approx$82.3\%), confirming faithful extraction; the
CLIP-ReID base value lands 1.5\,pp below its published 84.5\%, which we
attribute to the feature-branch choice in our re-extraction (the 512-D image
projection via the authors' released inference path)---a caveat that does not
affect the matched comparison, since the same features feed both the base and
post-processed numbers.

Our training recipe therefore confers no post-processing-independent advantage
over either baseline; if anything, both baselines' native descriptors re-rank
slightly better than ours. This is not a perfectly matched
feature-extraction comparison---the three native descriptors differ in
dimensionality and construction (3840-D global$+$JPM, 512-D CLIP projection,
1536-D concat-patch)---but the conclusion is robust to that caveat: the large
ViT-B retrieval numbers reported here, and the apparent post-TransReID progress
on this benchmark generally, are driven by post-processing rather than by
recipe or architectural refinement. We accordingly frame our single-stream
result as a reproducible, fully specified baseline, not a superior
recipe; the training-free fusion remains our primary quantitative advance.
