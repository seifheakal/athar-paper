\subsection{Cross-domain transfer and dataset bias}
Cross-dataset transfer is a known weak point of ReID systems. Unsupervised
domain adaptation (UDA) approaches such as SPGAN~\cite{deng2018spgan},
ECN~\cite{zhong2019ecn}, and UDAP~\cite{song2020udap} train on a labelled
source and adapt using unlabelled target data; transferred from the large
VehicleID source to VeRi-Wild they reach 24.1--34.7\% mAP on the
3{,}000-identity protocol, while the strongest reported \emph{direct} transfer
(no target data at all) reaches 37.7\% and full viewpoint-aware adaptation
39.7\%~\cite{zheng2020vapc}. Recent analysis attributes much of the gap between
in-domain and cross-domain performance to train/test similarity bias within the
ReID benchmarks themselves: models trained on VeRi-Wild lose
${\approx}15$\,pp mAP when evaluated on the visually similar VeRi-776, and mAP
on vehicle \emph{types} unseen during training drops from above 90\% to
65--70\% even for state-of-the-art models~\cite{benmabrouk2026genlimits}. This
context frames the cross-dataset evaluation of Section~\ref{sec:veriwild}:
large absolute drops under zero-shot transfer are the field norm, not an
artefact of a particular pipeline.
