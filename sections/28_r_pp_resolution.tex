\subsection{Post-processing contribution analysis}
Figure~\ref{fig:perstream} breaks down the per-stream contribution of each
post-processing step. Both streams begin at similar base performance
($\approx$82\% mAP); the larger relative gain of CLIP-SENet from AQE
($+6.87$\,pp vs.\ $+1.86$\,pp for ViT-B) reflects the broader
nearest-neighbour clusters of the ResNet-IBN feature space, which benefit more
from graph-based smoothing. The precise values are listed in the cumulative
block of Table~\ref{tab:ablation}.

\subsection{Input-resolution sensitivity}
\label{sec:res_sens}
Stream~1 trains and evaluates at 224\,$\times$\,224, whereas TransReID uses
256\,$\times$\,256 and CLIP-SENet 320\,$\times$\,320, raising the question of
whether the ViT-B stream leaves accuracy on the table at its lower resolution.
We measure the deployed checkpoint's \emph{inference}-resolution sensitivity by
re-extracting Stream~1 features at 256\,$\times$\,256 and 320\,$\times$\,320
(position embeddings bicubically interpolated to the larger patch grid) and
re-running the identical post-processing (Figure~\ref{fig:resolution}).
Accuracy degrades monotonically away from the native resolution: full-pipeline
mAP drops by $0.30$\,pp at 256 and $1.70$\,pp at 320. The checkpoint is thus
well matched to its training resolution, and higher-resolution
\emph{inference} is not an available lever; whether higher-resolution
\emph{training} would help (as in TransReID's 256 recipe) remains open and
would require retraining (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). The
native-resolution result doubles as a further independent reproduction: this
fresh extraction reproduces the headline 89.97\%\,/\,97.79\% operating point to
within 0.03\,pp.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.88\columnwidth]{Fig9}
