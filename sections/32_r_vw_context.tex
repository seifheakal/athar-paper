These numbers should be read against the cross-domain literature, not against
models trained on VeRi-Wild. Supervised training on VeRi-Wild's
30{,}671-identity training set yields 79--89\% mAP on this
protocol~\cite{lou2019veriwild,lu2025clipsenet,benmabrouk2026genlimits}, but
that is a different regime: it consumes the very labels whose absence transfer
measures. The appropriate peers are transfer results. Transferred from
VehicleID---a source with over twenty times more training identities than
VeRi-776---published UDA methods that additionally adapt on \emph{unlabelled
VeRi-Wild data} reach 24.1\% (SPGAN~\cite{deng2018spgan}), 30.0\%
(UDAP~\cite{song2020udap}), and 34.7\% (ECN~\cite{zhong2019ecn}) mAP on the
same 3{,}000-identity protocol; the strongest reported \emph{direct} transfer
(no target data) reaches 37.7\%, and full viewpoint-aware adaptation
39.7\%~\cite{zheng2020vapc}. Our zero-shot 35.93\% mAP (ViT-B $+$AQE), obtained
from the far smaller VeRi-776 source without touching any VeRi-Wild data, sits
inside this band---above several methods that do adapt on the target---and
remains inside it at the 5{,}000 and 10{,}000 protocols (30.5 and 23.8 vs.\
ECN's adapted 30.6 and 24.7), indicating the transferring stream is competitive
with cross-domain practice at every scale rather than an outlier.

This cross-dataset probe reframes the contributions honestly: the reproducible
ViT-B retrieval pipeline generalises in part, whereas the fusion's marginal
VeRi-776 advantage is domain-specific and should not be read as a transferable
gain. Reporting a benchmark on which the fusion \emph{underperforms} the
stronger stream is, we argue, more useful to the community than a curated
in-domain number.
