\noindent\textbf{Abstract}\\[4pt]
{\small
Transformer backbones dominate vehicle re-identification (Re-ID), yet their
training recipes and evaluation-time post-processing are rarely reported under
matched conditions, which makes it difficult to attribute reported progress to
any specific cause. This paper revisits the ViT-Base/16 pipeline on VeRi-776
and separates recipe effects from post-processing effects. A fully specified
recipe---CLIP-pretrained initialisation, AdamW with layer-wise learning-rate
decay, a tuned metric objective, and a three-step post-processing chain of
average query expansion, $k$-reciprocal re-ranking and flip test-time
augmentation---reaches 89.97\% mAP and 97.79\% Rank-1. A matched-condition
experiment shows that this figure is produced by post-processing rather than by
the recipe: at the raw-embedding level the recipe merely matches TransReID
(82.22 versus 82.30\% mAP), and applying the identical post-processing to the
official TransReID and CLIP-ReID checkpoints lifts them to 91.12 and 91.80\%
mAP, both above ours. We therefore present this stream as a reproducible
baseline, not a superior recipe. A training-free score-level fusion of this
stream with CLIP-SENet reaches 93.32\% mAP and 98.45\% Rank-1, exceeding the
best previously published mAP by 0.42\,pp, with a paired bootstrap confirming a
statistically significant gain over the stronger single stream. A zero-shot
transfer study across all three VeRi-Wild protocols shows the ViT-B embedding
transfers competitively while the fusion and re-ranking gains do not,
indicating that they are domain-specific. Checkpoints, notebooks and per-query
dumps are released for deterministic re-computation.
\par}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Motivation}
Vehicle re-identification is a core enabling technology for intelligent
transportation systems (ITS), supporting applications including urban traffic
surveillance, law enforcement, smart city infrastructure, and autonomous fleet
management~\cite{liu2016veri,liu2018provid}. The task requires matching the
same physical vehicle across multiple spatially distributed cameras whose
fields of view do not overlap, under conditions of substantial appearance
variation: the same vehicle may be observed from different angles, under
different illumination conditions, at varying resolutions, and after arbitrary
time intervals~\cite{liu2016veri}.

The VeRi-776 dataset~\cite{liu2016veri,liu2018provid}, introduced by Liu
\emph{et al.} at ICME 2016 and expanded through the PROVID framework published
in \emph{IEEE Transactions on Multimedia} in 2018, has become the standard
benchmark for vehicle ReID evaluation. It contains over 50\,000 images of 776
vehicles captured by 20 cameras covering a $1.0\,\text{km}^{2}$ urban area over
24 hours, with each vehicle appearing in 2 to 18 cameras under varied
viewpoints, illuminations, and occlusions~\cite{liu2016veri,liu2018provid}. The
standard evaluation protocol employs 37\,781 training images of 576
identities, 11\,579 gallery images, and 1\,678 query images across 200 test
identities~\cite{liu2018provid}. Performance is measured by mean Average
Precision (mAP) and Rank-1 Cumulative Matching Characteristic (CMC).

\subsection{The ViT-B training recipe gap}
The introduction of TransReID~\cite{he2021transreid} marked a turning point in
vehicle ReID by applying a pure Vision Transformer
(ViT)~\cite{dosovitskiy2021vit} backbone to the task. TransReID augments the
standard ViT-B/16 architecture with two purpose-built modules: the Side
Information Embedding (SIE), which encodes camera index and viewpoint as
learnable token offsets to reduce domain bias, and the Jigsaw Patch Module
(JPM), which shuffles patch tokens in a parallel branch to force locally robust
feature learning~\cite{he2021transreid}. With a DeiT-B/16 backbone and SGD
(momentum 0.9, LR 0.008, cosine decay), TransReID reports 82.3\% mAP and 97.1\%
Rank-1 on VeRi-776~\cite{he2021transreid}.

Despite broad adoption of this architecture as a baseline, subsequent work has
primarily focused on augmenting it with new architectural
components---semantic alignment via language
models~\cite{li2023clipreid,lu2025clipsenet}, graph-based spatial
reasoning~\cite{shen2022hpgn}, or multi-branch CNN
structures~\cite{luo2023mbr4b}---rather than systematically revisiting what a
well-specified ViT-B training-and-post-processing pipeline leaves on the table.
No pure ViT-B method has been reported above 90\% mAP on VeRi-776; we push a
pure ViT-B/16 pipeline to 89.97\% mAP---approaching that mark---and, via
training-free fusion with a complementary CNN-semantic stream, reach 93.3\%.
Crucially, we also test what actually produces that number, and report a result
that constrains our own claims.

\subsection{Contributions}
The contributions of this work are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{C1---Reproducible ViT-B/16 pipeline and a
    recipe-versus-post-processing analysis.} We release a fully specified
    ViT-B/16 retrieval pipeline (CLIP initialisation, a tuned metric objective,
    an optimised augmentation pipeline, AQE $+$ $k$-reciprocal
    re-ranking~\cite{zhong2017rerank}, flip TTA) reaching \textbf{89.97\% mAP /
    97.79\% Rank-1} on VeRi-776. Crucially, we test \emph{what} drives this
    number: applying our identical post-processing to the official TransReID
    and CLIP-ReID checkpoints lifts them to \textbf{91.12\%} and
    \textbf{91.80\% mAP}---both slightly \emph{above} ours---while all three
    are within 0.9\,pp without post-processing
    (82.11\,/\,82.99\,/\,82.22\%; Table~\ref{tab:matched}). Our recipe thus
    confers no advantage over these baselines at matched conditions: the ViT-B
    retrieval gains are attributable to post-processing, and we position
    Stream~1 as a reproducible baseline rather than a superior recipe. Among
    single models reported \emph{with} post-processing, MBR4B-LAI
    (92.1\% mAP~\cite{luo2023mbr4b}) is also higher.
  \item \textbf{C2---Marginal new best mAP via training-free fusion.}
    Score-level fusion with CLIP-SENet~\cite{lu2025clipsenet} achieves
    \textbf{93.32\% mAP / 98.45\% Rank-1}, $+0.42$\,pp mAP above the best
    previously published result (92.9\%~\cite{lu2025clipsenet}) and
    $+1.88$\,pp above our own reproduction of that stream (91.44\%). Rank-1
    (98.45\%) is competitive with but does not exceed CLIP-SENet standalone
    Rank-1 (98.7\%). Because the $+0.42$\,pp margin is small, is obtained over
    a concurrent unrefereed preprint, and reuses that model's checkpoint, we
    frame it as a demonstration of feature complementarity---which a paired
    bootstrap confirms is statistically significant over the best single
    stream (95\% CI $[1.39, 2.37]$\,pp mAP; Section~\ref{sec:holdout})---rather
    than a decisive state of the art.
  \item \textbf{C3---Consistent-protocol ablation path.} A comprehensive
    ablation traces the complete performance trajectory from the 82.3\%
    TransReID baseline to 93.3\% mAP under a single consistent post-processing
    protocol, separating post-processing contributions from recipe
    contributions rather than conflating them.
  \item \textbf{C4---Reproducible public release with validation and cost
    analysis.} All checkpoints (SHA-256 pinned), evaluation notebooks,
    configuration files, per-query evaluation dumps, and the post-processing
    pipeline are publicly released. Every reported number is the deterministic
    re-evaluation of a frozen, hash-pinned checkpoint and is thus
    \emph{exactly} re-computable from the release; from-scratch retraining is
    reproducible within a $\pm0.55$\,pp seed band
    (Section~\ref{sec:seed_variance}). Headline numbers use hyperparameters
    selected on the standard evaluation split---disclosed, not implicit---with
    held-out validation bounding the resulting optimism to $\leq$0.2\,pp mAP
    (Section~\ref{sec:holdout}), and the latency and memory of every pipeline
    stage---extraction, TTA, AQE, re-ranking, fusion---are measured for
    deployment planning (Section~\ref{sec:cost}).
  \item \textbf{C5---Cross-dataset transfer study.} A zero-shot
    VeRi-776$\rightarrow$VeRi-Wild evaluation across all three official test
    protocols (with paired-bootstrap CIs and released per-query dumps) shows
    the ViT-B stream transfers competitively with published cross-domain
    methods that draw on over twenty times more source identities or adapt on
    unlabelled target data, while the two-stream fusion does \emph{not}
    transfer at any scale---evidence, corroborated by a CityFlowV2 MTMC port,
    that its VeRi-776 advantage is domain-specific
    (Section~\ref{sec:veriwild}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{CNN-era vehicle re-identification}
The VeRi-776 benchmark was introduced by Liu et al.~\cite{liu2016veri} and
extended with the PROVID framework~\cite{liu2018provid}, which combined visual
appearance, licence plate recognition, camera topology, and spatiotemporal
cues, achieving 53.42\% mAP and 81.56\% Rank-1.\footnote{The PROVID
(53.42/81.56) and VAMI (50.13/77.03) figures are the values reported in the
respective papers and reproduced throughout the survey
literature~\cite{ye2022survey,li2024vehiclereidsurvey}.} A broad survey of deep
learning approaches to person and vehicle ReID is provided by Ye et
al.~\cite{ye2022survey}, while Li et al.~\cite{li2024vehiclereidsurvey} survey
vehicle-specific advances including part-level modelling, multi-source
training, and cross-domain adaptation.

VAMI~\cite{zhou2018vami} (CVPR 2018) synthesised target viewpoints via
adversarial training, reaching 50.13\% mAP and 77.03\% Rank-1.
PVEN~\cite{meng2020pven} (CVPR 2020) introduced parsing-based view-aware
embeddings via semantic segmentation for part-level feature extraction,
achieving 79.5\% mAP and 95.6\% Rank-1. CAL~\cite{rao2021cal} (ICCV 2021)
proposed counterfactual attention learning to suppress spurious background
cues, reaching 74.3\% mAP and 95.4\% Rank-1.
VehicleNet~\cite{zheng2020vehiclenet} (IEEE TMM 2020) demonstrated
multi-source data aggregation across VeRi-776, VehicleID, and synthetic data,
achieving 83.41\% mAP and 96.78\% Rank-1. HPGN~\cite{shen2022hpgn} (IEEE T-ITS
2022) modelled spatial significance across image scales via a pyramid graph
network, achieving 80.18\% mAP and 96.72\% Rank-1. A Strong
Baseline~\cite{cybercore2021strong} (CVPR-W AICity 2021) combined
ResNet101-IBN-a with multi-head attention for multi-domain training, reaching
87.1\% mAP and establishing a competitive ResNet-family ceiling prior to
transformer approaches. Khorramshahi et
al.~\cite{khorramshahi2023selfsupervision} later revisited scalable vehicle
ReID through self-supervised pretraining across ResNet, ViT, Swin, and ConvNeXt
backbones in the AI City setting.

\subsection{Transformer-based vehicle re-identification}
TransReID~\cite{he2021transreid} (ICCV 2021) was the first pure Vision
Transformer framework for object re-identification, introducing the SIE and JPM
modules and achieving 82.3\% mAP and 97.1\% Rank-1 on VeRi-776 with SGD
optimisation. As we demonstrate, switching the initialisation from ImageNet-21k
to CLIP~\cite{radford2021clip} and the optimiser from SGD to
AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2019adamw} yields a substantial gain \emph{within our own
development trajectory} without any architectural change---though, as
Section~\ref{sec:matched} shows, not a net gain over the tuned published
baseline.

CLIP-ReID~\cite{li2023clipreid} (AAAI 2023) introduced a two-stage
prompt-tuning procedure that exploits CLIP's vision-language representations
without requiring text annotations, achieving 84.5\% mAP and 97.3\% Rank-1 on
VeRi-776~\cite{opencodepapers2024veri}. CLIP-ReID uses the same ViT-B/16 CLIP
backbone family as the present work. Our full pipeline reaches 89.97\% mAP, but
we note this is not a matched comparison: CLIP-ReID's 84.5\% is reported
without post-processing, and at the raw-embedding level it in fact exceeds our
recipe (84.5\% vs.\ 82.2\%). The difference in the pipeline number is
post-processing---AQE $+$ $k$-reciprocal re-ranking $+$ TTA---which we apply
and the CLIP-ReID figure does not include (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).
Recent transformer variants have broadened this line beyond single-domain
VeRi-style retrieval: MSFFT~\cite{holla2024msfft} fuses multi-scale transformer
features for cross-platform vehicle ReID, while
MiKeCoCo~\cite{kuang2025mikecoco} uses CLIP-guided prompt knowledge for
generalisable vehicle ReID.

RPTM~\cite{ghosh2023rptm} (WACV 2023) proposed relation-preserving triplet
mining to stabilise triplet loss training across hard and semi-hard negatives,
achieving 88.0\% mAP and 97.3\% Rank-1. MBR4B-LAI~\cite{luo2023mbr4b} (ITSC
2023) combined four convolutional branches with location-aware identity
embeddings and camera/pose metadata, reaching 92.09\% mAP and 98.03\% Rank-1
after re-ranking---a competitive CNN ensemble that our fusion method surpasses.
FastReID~\cite{wang2020fastreid} provided a unified, production-quality
baseline toolbox widely used for ablation and comparison across vehicle and
person ReID benchmarks.

CLIP-SENet~\cite{lu2025clipsenet} (arXiv 2025) is a single-stream approach that
combines ResNet101-IBN-a local appearance features with 512-dimensional
semantic embeddings from a TinyCLIP Semantic Extraction Module (SEM) and an
Adaptive Fine-Grained Enhancement Module (AFEM), achieving 92.9\% mAP and
98.7\% Rank-1 on VeRi-776. This is the closest prior result to ours; the
present work takes the pre-trained CLIP-SENet v6 checkpoint as a frozen second
stream in a training-free score-level fusion, adding $+0.42$\,pp mAP above the
CLIP-SENet standalone result.

\subsection{Cross-domain transfer and dataset bias}
Cross-dataset transfer is a known weak point of ReID systems. Unsupervised
domain adaptation (UDA) approaches such as SPGAN~\cite{deng2018spgan},
ECN~\cite{zhong2019ecn}, and UDAP~\cite{song2020udap} train on a labelled
source and adapt using unlabelled target data; transferred from the large
VehicleID source to VeRi-Wild they reach 24.1--34.7\% mAP on the
3{,}000-identity protocol, while the strongest reported \emph{direct} transfer
(no target data at all) reaches 37.7\% and full viewpoint-aware adaptation
39.7\%~\cite{zheng2020vapc}. Recent analysis attributes much of the gap between
in-domain and cross-domain performance to train/test similarity bias within the
ReID benchmarks themselves: models trained on VeRi-Wild lose
${\approx}15$\,pp mAP when evaluated on the visually similar VeRi-776, and mAP
on vehicle \emph{types} unseen during training drops from above 90\% to
65--70\% even for state-of-the-art models~\cite{benmabrouk2026genlimits}. This
context frames the cross-dataset evaluation of Section~\ref{sec:veriwild}:
large absolute drops under zero-shot transfer are the field norm, not an
artefact of a particular pipeline.

\subsection{Re-ranking and post-processing}
The $k$-reciprocal encoding method by Zhong et al.~\cite{zhong2017rerank} (CVPR
2017) leverages the observation that if a gallery image lies in the
$k$-reciprocal nearest neighbours of a probe, it is more likely to be a true
match. A Jaccard-distance-based score is blended with the original cosine
distance, improving retrieval precision without requiring labelled data. Zhang
et al.~\cite{zhang2022vehiclererank} extended this idea specifically for
vehicle ReID by incorporating orientation-guided query expansion, showing that
viewpoint-consistent neighbour selection further improves re-ranking for highly
viewpoint-sensitive queries. Moral et al.~\cite{moral2023syntheticrerank} also
studied vehicle-specific post-processing, including gallery video-clip
information in the re-ranking step on CityFlow-ReID and VeRi-776.

Average Query Expansion (AQE)~\cite{arandjelovic2012aqe} replaces each query
feature with the mean of its top-$k$ retrieved gallery features, smoothing the
query manifold and consistently improving mAP at negligible computational cost.
Database-side Augmentation (DBA) applies the same expansion to gallery features
and can be combined with AQE; we evaluate AQE alone as it provides the best
mAP--Rank-1 trade-off at $k{=}3$ for our ViT-B stream.

Supervised Contrastive Learning (SupCon)~\cite{khosla2020supcon} extends
contrastive losses to the supervised setting by treating all same-class samples
as positives within each mini-batch, producing more uniformly distributed and
discriminative embedding spaces than triplet loss alone~\cite{ye2022survey}. We
evaluate SupCon as an alternative metric objective for Stream~1 (variant
A5$\beta$); the deployed checkpoint (A5$\alpha$) retains a triplet $+$ centre
objective, and the two are shown to be statistically equivalent
(Section~\ref{sec:seed_variance}).

\subsection{Positioning}
To the best of the authors' knowledge, no prior work reports above 90\% mAP on
VeRi-776 using a pure ViT-B backbone; the best prior pure-ViT result stands at
84.5\% mAP~\cite{li2023clipreid,opencodepapers2024veri}, reported without
post-processing. Our ViT-B \emph{retrieval pipeline} reaches 89.97\% mAP, but
we caution that this is not a like-for-like comparison: 84.5\% is a
raw-embedding number, whereas 89.97\% includes AQE $+$ $k$-reciprocal
re-ranking $+$ TTA. At matched no-post-processing conditions our embedding
(82.2\% mAP) does not exceed CLIP-ReID, and the matched comparison confirms the
pipeline number is a post-processing effect: the official TransReID and
CLIP-ReID checkpoints under our identical post-processing reach 91.12\% and
91.80\% mAP---both above our 89.97\%---so our recipe holds no advantage over
either baseline (Section~\ref{sec:matched}). For the fusion result, no prior
two-stream method exceeds 93.3\% mAP on VeRi-776; the best prior single-stream
result stands at 92.9\% mAP~\cite{lu2025clipsenet}, which our fusion exceeds by
a narrow $+0.42$\,pp.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}
% =============================================================================

Figure~\ref{fig:arch} gives the overall architecture of the two-stream
pipeline, from image input through per-stream feature extraction and
post-processing to score-level fusion, together with the two operating points
reported in this paper.

\begin{figure*}[!t]
  \centering

\subsection{Problem formulation}
Given a query image $q$ and a gallery set
$\mathcal{G} = \{g_1, g_2, \ldots, g_N\}$, the task is to retrieve all gallery
images depicting the same vehicle identity as the query and rank them above
non-matching images. Performance is measured by mAP and Rank-1 CMC. The
standard VeRi-776 evaluation protocol~\cite{liu2018provid} employs 1\,678 query
images and 11\,579 gallery images across 200 test identities; training uses
37\,781 images of 576 identities. All reported results strictly follow this
protocol.

\subsection{Stream~1: improved ViT-B training recipe}

\subsubsection{Backbone architecture}
The backbone follows the TransReID architecture~\cite{he2021transreid}: a
ViT-Base/16 augmented with the SIE module and the JPM. The SIE module encodes
VeRi-776's 20 camera indices as learnable additive offsets to the patch token
sequence. The JPM operates as a parallel branch that randomly shuffles groups
of patch tokens and applies a separate classification head. A BNNeck is applied
on the 768-dimensional global CLS token before the classification
head~\cite{he2021transreid}.

The key deviation from the original TransReID recipe is the backbone
initialisation: \texttt{vit\_base\_\allowbreak patch16\_\allowbreak clip\_\allowbreak
224.openai} (OpenAI CLIP pretrained ViT-B/16 via
\texttt{timm}~\cite{radford2021clip}) replaces
ImageNet-21k-only weights. CLIP pretraining provides richer visual-semantic
representations that transfer more effectively to fine-grained vehicle
appearance.

\subsubsection{Loss function}
Training employs a composite loss. The classification loss is cross-entropy
with label smoothing ($\varepsilon = 0.1$):
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE\text{-}LS}} =
    -\sum_{c=1}^{C} y_c^{\mathrm{smooth}} \log \hat{p}_c, \;\;
    y_c^{\mathrm{smooth}} = (1{-}\varepsilon)y_c + \varepsilon/C
  \label{eq:cels}
\end{equation}
where $C = 576$ training identities. The \emph{deployed} headline checkpoint
(A5$\alpha$) pairs this classification loss with a soft-margin triplet loss
(margin $m{=}0.3$) and a delayed centre loss ($\lambda{=}5\times10^{-4}$,
enabled after epoch~30), under layer-wise learning-rate decay 0.75:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{A5\alpha}} =
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE\text{-}LS}}
    + \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{tri}}(m{=}0.3)
    + \lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{centre}}
  \label{eq:a5alpha}
\end{equation}
As a statistically equivalent alternative, we also evaluate a
supervised-contrastive variant (A5$\beta$) that replaces the triplet and centre
terms with Supervised Contrastive Loss (SupCon)~\cite{khosla2020supcon}
($\tau = 0.07$, LLRD 0.65):
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SupCon}} =
    \sum_{i \in \mathcal{B}}
      \frac{-1}{|\mathcal{P}(i)|}
      \sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}(i)}
        \log
        \frac{\exp(\mathbf{z}_i \cdot \mathbf{z}_p / \tau)}
             {\sum_{a \in \mathcal{A}(i)} \exp(\mathbf{z}_i \cdot \mathbf{z}_a / \tau)}
  \label{eq:supcon}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{P}(i)$ is the set of positive pairs and $\mathcal{A}(i)$ all
other mini-batch samples, giving
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{A5\beta}} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE\text{-}LS}} +
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SupCon}}$. A5$\alpha$ and A5$\beta$ are evaluated as
separate ablation rows. The ablation campaign measured A5$\alpha$ at 89.06\%
mAP and A5$\beta$ at 88.57\% mAP under stream-1-only AQE$+$rerank evaluation;
the 0.49\,pp difference (A5$\alpha$ higher) falls within the $\pm 0.55$\,pp
three-seed standard deviation of A5$\alpha$ (88.75 $\pm$ 0.55\,pp mAP; see
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} and Section~\ref{sec:seed_variance}). Because the
difference falls well inside this three-seed standard deviation, the two
recipes are statistically equivalent and A5$\alpha$---the actually deployed
checkpoint---is retained as the headline Stream~1 recipe.

CircleLoss produced numerical instability (diverging training loss) in our
full-recipe training runs and is therefore excluded from the final recipe. To
probe the cause, we ran a controlled 400-step diagnostic with a minimal head
(plain CLIP-initialised ViT-B $+$ BNNeck $+$ CE-LS $+$ metric loss,
$P{\times}K=12{\times}8$) across five conditions: CircleLoss at
$\gamma\in\{32,64\}$ under fp16 and fp32, an ImageNet-initialised control, and
a triplet$+$centre control. \emph{None} of the CircleLoss conditions diverged
in this minimal setting---fp16 and fp32 differed only in descent speed, and
CLIP versus ImageNet initialisation behaved similarly---which does \emph{not}
support a simple precision- or initialisation-based explanation. The
instability we observed is therefore specific to the full recipe configuration
(SIE/JPM heads, layer-wise learning-rate decay, and the longer schedule) rather
than to CircleLoss on CLIP features per se; its precise cause remains
unisolated, and CircleLoss remains excluded on the empirical grounds observed
in the full setting. The probe script and loss curves are released with the
code.

\subsection{Stream~2: CLIP-SENet branch}
The CLIP-SENet architecture~\cite{lu2025clipsenet} provides a complementary
feature stream. Where the ViT-B processes images as sequences of global patch
tokens through self-attention, CLIP-SENet processes images through a
ResNet101-IBN-a backbone~\cite{pan2018ibn} that applies local convolutional
filters with Instance-Batch Normalisation (IBN), capturing fine-grained local
texture patterns less salient in global attention maps.

The CLIP-SENet architecture consists of three
components~\cite{lu2025clipsenet}:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{CNN backbone:} ResNet101-IBN-a~\cite{pan2018ibn} for
    2048-dimensional local appearance features. IBN balances instance
    normalisation (style invariance) and batch normalisation (discriminative
    learning), benefiting cross-camera ReID where camera-specific styles
    constitute nuisance variation.
  \item \textbf{Semantic Extraction Module (SEM):} the TinyCLIP image encoder
    extracts a 512-dimensional visual-semantic representation; unlike
    CLIP-ReID~\cite{li2023clipreid}, no text encoder is used at any
    stage~\cite{lu2025clipsenet}.
  \item \textbf{Adaptive Fine-Grained Enhancement Module (AFEM):} adaptively
    groups and weights fine-grained vehicle attributes (model, colour, shape)
    from TinyCLIP, producing robust semantic embeddings without text
    annotations~\cite{lu2025clipsenet}.
\end{enumerate}

The CLIP-SENet v6 checkpoint is trained on VeRi-776 with Adam
($\mathrm{lr}\!=\!5\!\times\!10^{-4}$, wd$=\!5\!\times\!10^{-4}$, cosine
schedule), $P\!=\!8$, $K\!=\!8$, effective batch 128 (\texttt{accum\_steps}=2),
CE-LS $+$ SupCon, 24 epochs with 5-epoch warm-up on a P100 GPU
($\approx$4\,h\,26\,min). Input size is $320\!\times\!320$, matching the
released CLIP-SENet~v6 checkpoint; a $256\!\times\!256$
\emph{training}-resolution variant was not reproduced in this campaign.

\subsection{Post-processing pipeline}
Post-processing is applied independently to each stream before fusion.

\subsubsection{Average query expansion}
For each query $\mathbf{q}$, the top-$k$ gallery features by cosine similarity
are averaged with the original query:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{AQE}} =
    \frac{1}{k{+}1}\!\left(\mathbf{q} + \sum_{i=1}^{k} \mathbf{g}_{\sigma(i)}\right)
  \label{eq:aqe}
\end{equation}
Stream~1 uses $k{=}3$; Stream~2 uses $k{=}10$, both selected by grid search
over $k\in\{1,3,5,10,20\}$. Stream~1's compact CLS token space is optimally
expanded with a tighter neighbourhood ($k{=}3$); Stream~2's broader ResNet-IBN
clusters benefit from a larger average ($k{=}10$), consistent with the IBN
design rationale for cross-camera style robustness~\cite{pan2018ibn}.

\subsubsection{$k$-reciprocal re-ranking}
The final distance for each query--gallery pair is:
\begin{equation}
  d_{\mathrm{final}}(q,g) =
    (1{-}\lambda)\,d_{\mathrm{Jaccard}}(q,g)
    + \lambda\,d_{\mathrm{cosine}}(q,g)
  \label{eq:rerank}
\end{equation}
Stream~1 (mAP-optimal): $k_1{=}80$, $k_2{=}15$, $\lambda{=}0.2$. Stream~1
(Rank-1-optimal): $k_1{=}24$, $k_2{=}8$, $\lambda{=}0.2$. Stream~2:
$k_1{=}50$, $k_2{=}10$, $\lambda{=}0.1$. Parameters were selected by exhaustive
grid search over $k_1\in\{20,22,24,25,26,28,30,50,80\}$,
$k_2\in\{6,7,8,9,10,15\}$, $\lambda\in\{0.15,0.18,0.2,0.22,0.25,0.3\}$.

\subsubsection{Concat-patch and flip test-time augmentation}
Features are extracted under original and horizontally flipped inputs and
concatenated with patch mean pooling (CLS $+$ mean patch tokens
$\to$ 1536D). This configuration yields the best-mAP operating point (89.97\%
mAP and 97.79\% Rank-1), while the single-flip CLS-only 768D configuration
yields the best-Rank-1 operating point (85.14\% mAP and 98.33\% Rank-1).

\subsection{Score-level fusion}
Given post-processed similarity matrices $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{ViT}}$ and
$\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{CLIP}}$, the fused similarity is:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{fusion}} =
    w_1\,\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{ViT}} + w_2\,\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{CLIP}},
  \quad w_1+w_2=1
  \label{eq:fusion}
\end{equation}
Concretely, the fusion pipeline uses the \textbf{1536D} concat-patch flip-TTA
Stream~1 representation (the best-mAP Stream~1 configuration of
Section~\ref{sec:method}, \emph{not} the 768D CLS-only variant) together with
the 2048D Stream~2 representation: AQE $k{=}3$ is applied to \emph{each}
stream, the resulting cosine-similarity matrices are combined via
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fusion}, and a \emph{single} joint $k$-reciprocal re-ranking
($k_1{=}80, k_2{=}15, \lambda{=}0.2$) is applied to the fused matrix. The
fusion therefore re-applies a common AQE $k{=}3$ to both streams; it does
\emph{not} reuse Stream~2's standalone AQE $k{=}10$ / re-ranking $50/10/0.1$,
which characterise the standalone CLIP-SENet result (91.44\% mAP) only.

This order of operations is empirically preferred, as
Figure~\ref{fig:order} illustrates: re-ranking each stream separately with its
standalone parameters and fusing the post-re-ranking distances instead yields
92.73\% mAP / 97.68\% Rank-1, $0.59$\,pp mAP below the deployed
fuse-then-jointly-re-rank order (93.32\%\,/\,98.45\%)---the joint
$k$-reciprocal neighbourhoods benefit from cross-stream consensus that
per-stream re-ranking discards.

\begin{figure*}[!t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Fig2}
  \caption{Order of operations in the fusion pipeline. \textbf{a} The deployed
  order applies a common AQE to both streams, fuses the similarity matrices and
  then re-ranks jointly. \textbf{b} The alternative order re-ranks each stream

  discards cross-stream consensus in the $k$-reciprocal neighbourhoods and
  costs 0.59\,pp mAP. Values are mAP\,/\,Rank-1 in \%}
  \label{fig:order}
\end{figure*}

Score-level fusion is preferred over feature-level fusion because the two
streams have fundamentally different feature geometries (1536D concat-patch ViT
representation vs.\ 2048D convolutional map) and normalisation schemes;
combining at the feature level would require a learned projection prone to
overfitting on 576 training identities. The streams also use different input
resolutions (224\,$\times$\,224 vs.\ 320\,$\times$\,320, each matching its
checkpoint); score-level fusion is agnostic to this mismatch by construction,
and harmonising would actively hurt---evaluating Stream~1 at
320\,$\times$\,320 costs $1.7$\,pp mAP (Section~\ref{sec:res_sens}).

We also verified the score-versus-feature-level choice empirically. Under the
identical downstream post-processing, plain L2-normalised concatenation of the
two streams reaches 93.08\% mAP / 98.63\% Rank-1, and \emph{weight-matched}
concatenation ($[\sqrt{w_1}\,\mathbf{f}_1;\sqrt{w_2}\,\mathbf{f}_2]$, the
feature-space counterpart of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fusion}) reaches 93.37\% /
98.51\%---statistically indistinguishable from the deployed score fusion
(93.32\% / 98.45\%)---whereas PCA-whitening (1024-D) fitted strictly on the
training split degrades sharply to 86.50\%. Feature- and score-level fusion are
therefore equivalent here when equally weighted, with no evidence that
feature-level concatenation overfits; we retain score-level fusion for its
interpretability (explicit stream weights) and because classical
whitening-style feature calibration actively hurts in this regime. The optimal
weights $w_1{=}0.35$, $w_2{=}0.65$ were found by grid search on the VeRi-776
query/gallery split ($\approx$49.5\,min on T4). Since no separate held-out
validation split is used for weight selection, the chosen weights may reflect a
mild optimistic bias. However, Figure~\ref{fig:weight} demonstrates that mAP
remains above 93.0\% across $w_1 \in [0.2,\,0.5]$, indicating that the optimal
weight is not a sharp peak and that the practical impact of this selection
procedure on the reported result is negligible.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:reproducibility}
Every number in this paper is produced by \emph{deterministic} evaluation from
two frozen, SHA-256-pinned checkpoints. We are careful about what this does and
does not guarantee: because inference---feature extraction, AQE,
$k$-reciprocal re-ranking, and score fusion---is deterministic, each reported
value \emph{re-evaluates} exactly from the released checkpoints. This is a
re-evaluation guarantee, not a claim that training is bit-for-bit reproducible;
the latter is bounded separately below. Table~\ref{tab:repro} lists the two
released checkpoints, their roles, and SHA-256 hashes.

We distinguish two reproducibility regimes. (i)~\textbf{Exact reproduction:}
evaluating the released checkpoints with the released notebooks re-computes
every table value exactly---a deterministic re-evaluation, not an independent
re-derivation---since inference is deterministic and the checkpoints are
hash-pinned; this is the operative guarantee for all headline numbers, and the
fusion headline was confirmed identical across three independent evaluations
(two GPU notebooks and a CPU recomputation from dumped features). As a stronger
check than deterministic re-evaluation, an \emph{independent re-extraction}---%
fresh forward passes through the two SHA-256-verified checkpoints on a separate
compute environment and account---reproduced the entire cumulative trajectory
of Table~\ref{tab:ablation} and the 89.97 / 91.44 / 93.32\% mAP headline to
within 0.01\,pp, confirming that the released checkpoints, not any cached
feature dump, determine the reported numbers. (ii)~\textbf{Within-variance
reproduction:} retraining a stream from scratch reproduces its recipe only
within the $\pm0.55$\,pp three-seed standard deviation
(Section~\ref{sec:seed_variance}), because GPU training is not bit-reproducible
(cuDNN autotuning, non-deterministic atomics) even under fixed seeds; the
released checkpoint---not a fresh retrain---therefore defines the headline.

From-scratch reproduction is further limited by an incomplete training-time
record: the $P\times K$ identity-sampling tuple was not logged (only the
effective batch size of 96 is confirmed; Section~\ref{sec:method}), so an exact
retraining configuration cannot be fully specified from our logs, which we flag
as a reproducibility limitation. The standard VeRi-776 protocol is asserted at
evaluation time ($1\,678$ query, $11\,579$ gallery, $576$ training identities);
all evaluation used PyTorch~2.4.1 (CUDA~12.4) on a single NVIDIA~T4 GPU. The
evaluation notebooks, the checkpoint manifest (with full hashes), and the
per-table provenance mapping are released in the project repository
(\url{https://github.com/MRKDaGods/gp}).

\subsection{Hyperparameter selection, held-out validation, and significance}
\label{sec:holdout}
We report all post-processing hyperparameters---AQE $k$, the $k$-reciprocal
re-ranking triple $(k_1,k_2,\lambda)$, and the fusion weight $w_1$---as
selected by grid search on the VeRi-776 query/gallery split, and we treat this
as a source of potential optimistic bias rather than leaving it implicit. To
state the protocol once and unambiguously: \emph{every headline number in this
paper uses these split-tuned values}; the validation analyses below quantify
and bound the optimism of that choice---they are audits of the headline
configuration, not its source.

Three observations bound that bias. First, the selection surface is flat, not
peaked: fused mAP stays above $93.0\%$ across the entire range
$w_1\in[0.2,0.5]$ (Figure~\ref{fig:weight}), so the reported result does not
sit on a sharp test-set optimum. Second, the re-ranking and AQE settings are
close to established defaults ($k_1{=}80,k_2{=}15,\lambda{=}0.2$ lie within the
ranges recommended by Zhong et al.~\cite{zhong2017rerank}; AQE $k{=}3$ is a
standard small neighbourhood), so they are not idiosyncratically fit to
VeRi-776. Third, the test set (200 identities, 1\,678 queries) is large
relative to the small number of scalar hyperparameters selected, limiting the
degrees of freedom available for overfitting.

To move beyond these a-priori arguments, we ran a disjoint-identity held-out
protocol: the 200 test identities are split into two 100-identity halves, the
fusion weight is selected on the first half (with AQE and re-ranking held at
the standard values above), and the result is reported on the second half with
that weight frozen. The validation half selects $w_1{=}0.25$ whereas the full
test set favours $w_1{=}0.35$; because the weight surface is flat
(Figure~\ref{fig:weight}), this disagreement is almost free---reporting the
full-test result at the validation-selected $w_1{=}0.25$ gives 93.25\% mAP,
only \textbf{0.07\,pp} below the test-tuned 93.32\%. The optimistic bias from
selecting the fusion weight on the evaluation set is therefore
$\lesssim$0.1\,pp and changes no conclusion; with that weight frozen, the
held-out 100-identity split reaches 96.3\% mAP / 99.1\% Rank-1, confirming the
fusion generalises across disjoint identities. The held-out-validation script
is released so readers can reproduce this check.

Because this split still draws its identities from the test set, we add a
second protocol using identities disjoint from the test set entirely: we carve
a 100-identity validation set from the 576 \emph{training} identities and
re-select the fusion weight there. As those images were seen during training,
retrieval on them saturates (mAP $=100\%$ for every $w_1\in[0.2,0.5]$), so the
selection is uninformative in isolation---but freezing the resulting choice
($w_1{=}0.2$) and evaluating on the true test set gives 93.13\% mAP, only
\textbf{0.18\,pp} below the test-tuned 93.32\%. Both protocols thus bound the
evaluation-set weight-selection bias below 0.2\,pp. For a selection-free
configuration out of the box, Table~\ref{tab:oppoints} lists the
validation-selected operating point ($w_1{=}0.25$, literature-default
post-processing) at 93.25\% mAP / 98.15\% Rank-1---the recommended default for
unbiased comparison. A fully unseen validation set---holding identities out of
\emph{training} rather than reusing seen images---would be stricter but
requires retraining, which we note as the rigorous alternative.

The post-processing parameters sit on the same kind of plateau. An
axis-aligned sweep around the deployed Stream-1 setting
($k_1\in\{40,80,120\}$, $k_2\in\{8,15,25\}$, $\lambda\in\{0.05,0.2,0.4\}$, one
factor varied at a time after AQE $k{=}3$) yields 88.3--90.1\% mAP across the
entire grid, with the deployed $(80,15,0.2)$ at 89.99\%---not even the grid
maximum (90.14\% at $\lambda{=}0.05$). AQE alone is likewise well-behaved: mAP
rises monotonically with $k$ (82.3\,/\,84.2\,/\,85.4\,/\,87.4\% at
$k{=}1/3/5/10$) before re-ranking is applied. No reported number therefore
depends on a sharp, idiosyncratic post-processing optimum.

We also tested whether score calibration removes the need to tune the fusion
weight. Simple $z$-score calibration of each stream's similarity distribution
before an equal-weight ($w_1{=}0.5$) fusion does \emph{not} help---it lowers
mAP to 90.35\%---whereas the \emph{uncalibrated} equal-weight fusion already
reaches 93.10\%, within 0.22\,pp of the tuned 93.32\%. The fusion weight is
therefore barely consequential and needs no careful evaluation-set tuning;
calibration is unnecessary and, here, counter-productive.

We further quantify the significance of the fusion gain with a paired bootstrap
over the 1\,678 queries (5\,000 resamples). The margin over the stronger single
stream has a 95\% confidence interval excluding zero:
fusion~$-$~CLIP-SENet $=+1.88$\,pp mAP (95\% CI $[1.39, 2.37]$) and
fusion~$-$~ViT-B $=+3.34$\,pp (95\% CI $[2.81, 3.88]$), both measured against
our own same-condition reproductions of those streams; the complementarity
underlying the fusion is thus statistically robust. This test concerns the gain
over the streams we can evaluate---the narrower $+0.42$\,pp margin over the
\emph{published} CLIP-SENet mAP cannot be significance-tested without that
model's per-query results, and we do not claim it as significant. The fusion
weight itself is barely consequential: $w_1{=}0.35$ exceeds $w_1{=}0.30$ by
only $+0.10$\,pp (95\% CI $[0.02, 0.19]$). To let readers audit these tests
independently, we release the per-query average-precision values and the fused
distance matrix for the headline configuration, together with the script that
recomputes mAP/CMC and the bootstrap intervals from them.

\subsection{Input-resolution sensitivity}
\label{sec:res_sens}
Stream~1 trains and evaluates at 224\,$\times$\,224, whereas TransReID uses
256\,$\times$\,256 and CLIP-SENet 320\,$\times$\,320, raising the question of
whether the ViT-B stream leaves accuracy on the table at its lower resolution.
We measure the deployed checkpoint's \emph{inference}-resolution sensitivity by
re-extracting Stream~1 features at 256\,$\times$\,256 and 320\,$\times$\,320
(position embeddings bicubically interpolated to the larger patch grid) and
re-running the identical post-processing (Figure~\ref{fig:resolution}).
Accuracy degrades monotonically away from the native resolution: full-pipeline
mAP drops by $0.30$\,pp at 256 and $1.70$\,pp at 320. The checkpoint is thus
well matched to its training resolution, and higher-resolution
\emph{inference} is not an available lever; whether higher-resolution
\emph{training} would help (as in TransReID's 256 recipe) remains open and
would require retraining (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). The
native-resolution result doubles as a further independent reproduction: this
fresh extraction reproduces the headline 89.97\%\,/\,97.79\% operating point to
within 0.03\,pp.

\subsection{Cross-dataset generalisation on VeRi-Wild}
\label{sec:veriwild}
A central question for any VeRi-776 result is whether it reflects transferable
re-identification ability or dataset-specific tuning. To probe this, we
evaluate our VeRi-776-trained checkpoints \emph{zero-shot} on
VeRi-Wild~\cite{lou2019veriwild} (416{,}314 images, 40{,}671 identities, 174
cameras), applying the identical feature extraction and post-processing with
\emph{no} fine-tuning. VeRi-Wild's 174 cameras do not correspond to VeRi-776's
twenty, so no remapping is attempted: the Stream~1 camera side-information
embedding is fixed to a single constant index for all images (one shared camera
token, so the SIE offset is identical everywhere and cancels in relative
comparisons); disabling camera/view embeddings is standard practice in

Four observations follow (Table~\ref{tab:veriwild} and
Figure~\ref{fig:transfer}). First, the ViT-B stream \emph{transfers}, and does
so at every gallery scale: 31.7\,/\,26.7\,/\,20.9\% mAP raw and
35.9\,/\,30.5\,/\,23.8\% with AQE on the 3{,}000\,/\,5{,}000\,/\,10{,}000
protocols---far below its in-domain 89.97\% (as expected for a
40{,}671-identity in-the-wild target with no adaptation), but a consistent,
non-trivial signal that the CLIP-initialised ViT-B embedding is not merely fit
to VeRi-776. Its degradation with gallery scale ($-12.1$\,pp mAP from the
smallest to the largest protocol) parallels the published VehicleID-sourced
methods ($-11.4$\,pp for direct-transfer VAPC, $-10.0$\,pp for ECN),
indicating a shared gallery-size mechanism rather than a pipeline artefact.
Rank-1 trails the VehicleID-sourced methods by more than mAP does, consistent
with their source providing over twenty times more identities for top-1
discrimination.

Second, and more informative, the score-level \emph{fusion does not survive the
domain shift at any scale}: at the VeRi-776-tuned weight $w_1{=}0.35$ it
reaches 28.7\,/\,24.2\,/\,18.5\% mAP raw and 33.6\,/\,28.1\,/\,21.4\% with AQE,
\emph{below} the stronger single stream in both conditions at every protocol.
Under transfer the CLIP-SENet stream is substantially weaker
(20.2\,/\,16.8\,/\,12.2\% mAP), and the weight tuned on VeRi-776---which
weights that stream at $0.65$---drags the fusion below ViT-B alone. (The
table's fusion rows apply each stream's \emph{standalone} AQE setting
($k{=}3/k{=}10$) uniformly across scales; under the exactly deployed fusion
configuration---common AQE $k{=}3$ with joint re-ranking---the 3{,}000-protocol
fusion reaches 32.8\% mAP with AQE and 31.4\% with joint re-ranking, leading to
the same conclusion.) This directly corroborates the CityFlowV2 result noted in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}, where a direct port of the same fusion degraded
MTMC IDF1 by $\approx$1.8\,pp: the two-stream complementarity, and the weight
that governs it, are properties of the VeRi-776 domain rather than transferable
re-identification mechanisms.

Third, AQE---an $\mathcal{O}(N)$ operation---helps the transferring stream at
every scale ($+4.3$, $+3.7$, and $+2.9$\,pp mAP), while consistently
\emph{hurting} the weak stream's Rank-1, a known failure mode of query
expansion on poorly clustered features. Fourth, $k$-reciprocal re-ranking---the
largest single accuracy step in-domain ($+5.55$\,pp on
VeRi-776)---\emph{reduces} accuracy under transfer: measured at the 3{,}000
protocol, it lowers ViT-B from 35.9 to 34.8\% mAP (and Rank-1 by 3.3\,pp) and
the fusion from 33.6 to 30.9\%. On poorly clustered transfer features the
$k$-reciprocal neighbourhoods admit false matches that the Jaccard blending
then amplifies, so the in-domain post-processing recipe does not port
wholesale: like the fusion weight, re-ranking's benefit is domain-specific.
This is not an artefact of the default parameters: across the same
axis-aligned grid swept in-domain (Section~\ref{sec:holdout}), \emph{every}
re-ranked transfer configuration lands at 33.4--35.5\% mAP, below the 35.9\%
AQE-only result. AQE itself is near-optimal at the VeRi-776-tuned $k{=}3$: the
transfer-side optimum is $k{=}5$ (36.2\%), only $0.3$\,pp higher, and we
deliberately do not adopt target-tuned settings. Its measured cost is 254\,s
for the 41{,}861-image query$+$gallery set on CPU---roughly five times the
50\,s in-domain pass at $3.6\times$ the gallery size, consistent with the
$\mathcal{O}(N^{2})$ scaling discussed in Section~\ref{sec:cost}. Per-query AP
dumps for every transfer configuration are released alongside the VeRi-776
dumps (\texttt{scripts/eval/vw\_per\_query\_ap.npz}), so all reported CIs can
be recomputed independently.

The fusion failure, unlike the re-ranking failure, admits a training-free
remedy. We tested \emph{query-adaptive} weights computed from each stream's own
AQE'd score row with no target-domain information: a margin rule
$w_1^{(i)}{=}m_1/(m_1{+}m_2)$, $m_s{=}\max(s)-\mathrm{mean}(\text{top-}50)$,
and a top-100 dispersion analogue. At the 3{,}000 protocol the margin rule
recovers \textbf{35.9\%} mAP (from 32.8\% at the fixed weight; single stream
35.9\%) by shifting weight toward the reliable stream
($\bar{w}_1{\approx}0.63$). A constant-weight control attributes the rescue:
the transfer weight surface peaks at $w_1{\approx}0.8$/36.5\%---marginally
\emph{above} the single stream, so some complementarity survives the shift, but
locating that optimum requires target-domain evaluation---while a constant
$w_1{=}0.63$ scores 36.0\% and recentring the rule's weights on the optimum
adds nothing (36.4\%): the rule's value is finding a safe operating point
\emph{without labels}, not yet its per-query variation. In-domain the rules
cost 1.1--1.4\,pp vs.\ the tuned fixed weight (87.0--87.3 vs.\ 88.4\% mAP at
matched AQE-only settings) while staying well above the single stream
(84.2\%), so adaptive weighting acts as a domain-robustness guard. A
ground-truth per-query \emph{oracle} reaches 40.1\% mAP / 66.9\%
Rank-1---above every published direct-transfer entry in
Table~\ref{tab:veriwild}---so per-query headroom is real but uncaptured by
first-order score statistics; \emph{learned} query-adaptive fusion is thus a
concrete, quantified future direction.

These numbers should be read against the cross-domain literature, not against
models trained on VeRi-Wild. Supervised training on VeRi-Wild's
30{,}671-identity training set yields 79--89\% mAP on this
protocol~\cite{lou2019veriwild,lu2025clipsenet,benmabrouk2026genlimits}, but
that is a different regime: it consumes the very labels whose absence transfer
measures. The appropriate peers are transfer results. Transferred from
VehicleID---a source with over twenty times more training identities than
VeRi-776---published UDA methods that additionally adapt on \emph{unlabelled
VeRi-Wild data} reach 24.1\% (SPGAN~\cite{deng2018spgan}), 30.0\%
(UDAP~\cite{song2020udap}), and 34.7\% (ECN~\cite{zhong2019ecn}) mAP on the
same 3{,}000-identity protocol; the strongest reported \emph{direct} transfer
(no target data) reaches 37.7\%, and full viewpoint-aware adaptation
39.7\%~\cite{zheng2020vapc}. Our zero-shot 35.93\% mAP (ViT-B $+$AQE), obtained
from the far smaller VeRi-776 source without touching any VeRi-Wild data, sits
inside this band---above several methods that do adapt on the target---and
remains inside it at the 5{,}000 and 10{,}000 protocols (30.5 and 23.8 vs.\
ECN's adapted 30.6 and 24.7), indicating the transferring stream is competitive
with cross-domain practice at every scale rather than an outlier.

This cross-dataset probe reframes the contributions honestly: the reproducible
ViT-B retrieval pipeline generalises in part, whereas the fusion's marginal
VeRi-776 advantage is domain-specific and should not be read as a transferable
gain. Reporting a benchmark on which the fusion \emph{underperforms} the
stronger stream is, we argue, more useful to the community than a curated
in-domain number.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{What drives the ViT-B gain}
The improvement of our ViT-B pipeline over the published TransReID baseline
combines recipe and post-processing effects that the ablation deliberately
separates. Within the recipe, CLIP pretraining initialisation and AdamW with
layer-wise LR decay are the two ingredients our development depended on most
(A2, A4); CLIP provides a rich visual-semantic prior for fine-grained vehicle
discrimination, consistent with transfer-learning observations in the ReID
literature~\cite{li2023clipreid,radford2021clip}. We reiterate the caveat of
Section~\ref{sec:matched}: at matched no-post-processing conditions the recipe
does not by itself exceed TransReID (82.2\% vs.\ 82.3\% mAP), and under our
identical post-processing both TransReID and CLIP-ReID in fact surpass our
pipeline (91.12\% and 91.80\% vs.\ 89.97\%). These are therefore drivers
\emph{within our recipe} rather than net gains over a tuned baseline, and the
bulk of the reported improvement is contributed by AQE, $k$-reciprocal
re-ranking, and TTA. For context, a DINOv2-initialised
alternative~\cite{oquab2023dinov2} evaluated as a second fusion stream on
VeRi-776 did not match the CLIP-based CLIP-SENet stream (below 89.3\% mAP
standalone), so CLIP-family semantic supervision was retained for both streams;
we did not run a controlled DINOv2-versus-CLIP \emph{Stream-1 backbone} swap on
VeRi-776 and therefore make no claim about pretraining scale versus semantic
supervision from this benchmark alone.

\subsection{Why score-level fusion works}
The complementarity is architectural. The ViT-B backbone processes images as
global patch-token sequences with multi-head self-attention, producing
representations sensitive to holistic vehicle structure and long-range spatial
relationships~\cite{he2021transreid,dosovitskiy2021vit}. The CLIP-SENet
ResNet101-IBN-a backbone~\cite{pan2018ibn} applies hierarchical local
convolutional filters capturing fine-grained texture, with IBN providing
simultaneous style normalisation and discriminative training. The AFEM branch
injects attribute-level structure from TinyCLIP~\cite{lu2025clipsenet}
orthogonal to ViT patch attention. Score-level fusion preserves each stream's
post-processing calibration independently and requires no additional training.
The optimal $w_2{=}0.65$ for CLIP-SENet reflects its higher standalone
post-processed performance (91.44\% vs.\ 89.97\% mAP for ViT-B); the ViT-B
branch still contributes $+1.88$\,pp above CLIP-SENet alone, confirming genuine
complementarity.

\subsection{Backbone scale and overfitting}
ViT-L/14 (304\,M parameters) was trained under the same recipe as a
higher-capacity alternative. Despite its larger parameter count it overfit
VeRi-776's 576 training identities---a known failure mode at this dataset
scale---and did not exceed the ViT-B/16 CLIP result after re-ranking. This
supports prioritising systematic post-processing over backbone scaling as the
primary performance lever for small-scale ReID benchmarks.

\subsection{Computational cost and deployment}
\label{sec:cost}
Because deployment cost is a first-order concern for intelligent transportation
systems, we make the inference budget explicit and separate it from the
retrieval-accuracy budget.

\paragraph{Model and feature-extraction cost.} A single Stream~1 forward pass
is a ViT-B/16 at $224\times224$ ($\approx$86\,M parameters,
$\approx$17.6\,GFLOPs); Stream~2 (ResNet101-IBN-a $+$ TinyCLIP) is of
comparable order at its $320\times320$ input. The best-mAP Stream~1
configuration uses horizontal-flip TTA, which \emph{doubles} the forward
passes; the concat-patch representation reuses already-computed tokens and adds
negligible compute. The two-stream fusion requires \emph{both} backbones,
roughly doubling extraction time and model memory relative to a single stream.
Measured on a single NVIDIA~T4 (batch~1), a Stream~1 forward is 13.5\,ms/image
at 700\,MB peak VRAM, rising to 27.2\,ms with flip-TTA; Stream~2 is
21.2\,ms/image at 721\,MB; the full two-stream extraction is 48.4\,ms/image
($\approx$21 images/s), matching the expected $\approx$2$\times$ TTA and fusion
multipliers. All of these costs are $\mathcal{O}(N)$ in the number of images
and are trivially parallelisable. Feature storage is modest: 6.1\,kB/image for
Stream~1 (1536D fp32) and 8.2\,kB/image for Stream~2 (2048D), i.e.\ 166\,MB for
the full two-stream VeRi-776 gallery; even the 128{,}517-image VeRi-Wild
10{,}000-protocol gallery fits in 0.79\,GB fp32 (0.39\,GB fp16) for Stream~1.

\paragraph{Post-processing cost.} Post-processing is where cost concentrates.
AQE is a single similarity pass with a top-$k$ average and is cheap.
$k$-reciprocal re-ranking~\cite{zhong2017rerank}, by contrast, is
$\mathcal{O}(N^{2})$ in gallery size $N$ (here $N\!\approx\!11.6$\,k) in both
time and memory, and it is the dominant offline cost; it contributes the single
largest accuracy step ($+5.55$\,pp mAP on Stream~1) but, as written, is
unsuitable for low-latency online retrieval. Figure~\ref{fig:cost}a makes the
scaling concrete on VeRi-776: a single-query-set re-ranking pass over the full
11.6k gallery takes 50\,s and the CPU AQE pass 138\,s, both rising steeply as
the gallery grows. The sharp CPU-AQE jump between $N{=}8{,}684$ and
$N{=}11{,}579$ is a memory-hierarchy effect---the similarity matrix outgrows
cache and BLAS tiling degrades---not an algorithmic one: the chunked GPU AQE
used everywhere else in this paper scales smoothly to $N{=}138{,}517$.

Under domain transfer the picture changes in kind, not only in degree: on the
VeRi-Wild probe of Section~\ref{sec:veriwild} (query$+$gallery $=41{,}861$) a
re-ranking pass measures 254\,s on CPU---about $5\times$ the in-domain pass at
$3.6\times$ the gallery, consistent with $\mathcal{O}(N^{2})$ scaling---and it
\emph{reduces} accuracy on the poorly clustered transfer features ($-1.1$\,pp
mAP for ViT-B, $-2.7$\,pp for the fusion; Table~\ref{tab:veriwild}). A further
measured pass at $N{=}69{,}389$ (the 5{,}000-protocol union, with the
pairwise-distance state held in fp16 to fit host memory) takes
519\,s---consistent with quadratic-order growth over the 50\,s in-domain and
254\,s 3{,}000-protocol passes---and once again \emph{reduces} accuracy
(30.48\%$\rightarrow$29.42\% mAP). Beyond this point the memory wall arrives
before the time wall: at the VeRi-Wild 10{,}000-identity protocol
($N{=}138{,}517$) the $\mathcal{O}(N^{2})$ pairwise-distance state occupies
${\approx}77$\,GB in fp32 (${\approx}38$\,GB even in fp16)---beyond common host
memory---so the full-gallery measurement is infeasible for the \emph{dense}
implementation; chunked GPU AQE, by contrast, ran at every scale. Re-ranking is
thus doubly unattractive for cross-domain retrieval: its cost scales
quadratically and its accuracy benefit is domain-specific; AQE-only
post-processing is the sensible transfer configuration, and is what we headline
on VeRi-Wild.

\paragraph{A scalable sparse implementation.} The memory wall is a property of
the dense implementation, not of the algorithm. We benchmarked a sparse
top-$k$ variant that materialises only the first $k_1{+}1$ rank columns and
stores $V$ sparse ($\mathcal{O}(Nk_1)$ memory), otherwise following the
official computation (squared max-normalised distances, $2/3$-rule expansion,
LQE). In-domain it matches dense to 0.07\,pp (89.92 vs.\ 89.99\% mAP; Rank-1
97.91 vs.\ 97.79) while running \emph{faster} (32 vs.\ 44\,s) on a 7.9\,MB
working set versus 703\,MB dense. At $N{=}41{,}861$: 91\,s versus 254\,s
(34.03 vs.\ 34.85\% mAP, both below the 35.9\% AQE-only transfer result). At
$N{=}138{,}517$---where the dense state alone occupies 77\,GB---it
\emph{runs}: 372\,s with an 85\,MB sparse $V$, its AQE-only reference
reproducing Table~\ref{tab:veriwild} exactly (23.84\%). This disentangles the
two failure modes: the cost wall is removable by implementation, yet re-ranking
\emph{still reduces} transfer accuracy at this scale (22.22 vs.\ 23.84\%
mAP)---the domain-specific accuracy failure lies in the transferred feature
geometry, not the implementation. For in-domain deployment, the sparse variant
makes the $+5.5$\,pp re-ranking step practical at gallery scales where the
dense version is not. Figure~\ref{fig:cost}b summarises the comparison.

\paragraph{Accuracy/latency trade-offs.} These costs are separable levers. A
system that must run online can drop $k$-reciprocal re-ranking for
$\mathcal{O}(N)$ cosine retrieval at a cost of roughly $5.5$\,pp mAP, disable
flip-TTA ($\approx\!0.3$\,pp) to halve extraction, and use a single stream
rather than two-stream fusion (fusion contributes $+1.88$\,pp over the stronger
standalone stream) to halve model cost---recovering a real-time configuration
at a quantified accuracy penalty. For offline forensic search, where
VeRi-776-style batch evaluation is representative, the full pipeline is
appropriate. The per-image latencies above are measured values; a reproducible
micro-benchmark of latency, peak VRAM, and FLOPs for each component is released
with the code (\texttt{scripts/benchmark\_veri\_inference.py}).

\paragraph{Alternative re-ranking: CA-Jaccard.} Because re-ranking is central
to the reported gains, we additionally benchmarked the official CA-Jaccard
implementation~\cite{chen2024cajaccard} (CVPR 2024) as an evaluation-time
re-ranker on Stream-1. At its authors' evaluation defaults ($k_1{=}30$,
$k_2{=}6$, $\lambda{=}0.3$; CKRNN $k_1^{\mathrm{intra}}{=}5$,
$k_1^{\mathrm{inter}}{=}20$), CA-Jaccard reaches 86.95\% mAP / 98.39\% Rank-1
in-domain, versus 86.66\% / 97.56\% for plain $k$-reciprocal re-ranking run
through the same implementation at the same parameters: camera-awareness helps
at matched settings ($+0.29$\,pp mAP, $+0.83$\,pp Rank-1), and its Rank-1
exceeds even our tuned configuration's 97.79\%. Its mAP nevertheless remains
3.0\,pp below our tuned standard $k$-reciprocal (89.99\%)---a
defaults-versus-tuned comparison, disclosed as such. Under zero-shot transfer
the picture does not improve: on VeRi-Wild (3{,}000 protocol) CA-Jaccard scores
34.88\% mAP / 52.9\% Rank-1---no better than tuned standard $k$-reciprocal
(34.85\%) and below AQE-only (35.93\%, Rank-1 60.3\%)---while plain
$k$-reciprocal at CA-Jaccard's milder defaults ($k_1{=}30$, $\lambda{=}0.3$) is
approximately neutral (36.03\%, within the AQE-only confidence interval). No
re-ranking variant we tested retains its in-domain benefit under domain shift,
indicating the failure mode is the transferred feature geometry rather than
camera-unawareness. (The $N{=}41{,}861$ transfer runs use a memory-lean port of
the official implementation, validated to reproduce it exactly in-domain; the
port is released.) We did not test orientation-guided
expansion~\cite{zhang2022vehiclererank} or database-side multi-view aggregation
under this pipeline; the released float16 feature dumps make such comparisons
possible on CPU without re-running extraction.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The full post-processing pipeline (AQE $+$ re-ranking) has
    $\mathcal{O}(N^{2})$ complexity in gallery size $N$ in its dense form and
    is primarily suited to offline evaluation rather than real-time deployment;
    the sparse implementation of Section~\ref{sec:cost} removes the memory wall
    but not the quadratic time growth.
  \item The fusion strategy requires two inference passes, approximately
    doubling memory and latency relative to a single-stream model.
  \item Cross-dataset transfer is reported in Section~\ref{sec:veriwild}:
    applied zero-shot to all three VeRi-Wild protocols, the ViT-B embedding
    transfers in part (35.9\,/\,30.5\,/\,23.8\% mAP with AQE at
    3{,}000\,/\,5{,}000\,/\,10{,}000 identities, competitive with published
    VehicleID-sourced transfer), but the two-stream fusion \emph{does not}
    generalise---at the VeRi-776-tuned weight it falls \emph{below} the
    stronger single stream at every scale---and $k$-reciprocal re-ranking
    likewise reduces transfer accuracy. This corroborates the CityFlowV2
    evidence, where a direct port of the same fusion produced monotonic MTMC
    IDF1 degradation, with a best-case change of approximately $-1.8$\,pp at
    higher fusion weights. Together these indicate that the fusion's marginal
    VeRi-776 advantage is domain-specific. A first mitigation is demonstrated
    in Section~\ref{sec:veriwild}: training-free query-adaptive weighting
    recovers single-stream transfer accuracy (35.9 vs.\ 32.8\% mAP fixed), and
    a ground-truth per-query oracle (40.1\%) quantifies the headroom for
    \emph{learned} query-adaptive fusion, which we leave to future work.
    Absolute VeRi-Wild accuracy is also low in this no-adaptation setting, and
    no fine-tuned cross-dataset numbers are reported.
  \item Our evaluation follows the standard random-identity splits of VeRi-776
    and VeRi-Wild. Recent analysis shows these splits contain many visually
    near-duplicate identities across train and test, and that mAP on vehicle
    \emph{types} held out of training drops sharply (from above 90\% to
    65--70\%) even for state-of-the-art models~\cite{benmabrouk2026genlimits}.
    We did not evaluate under such a seen/unseen-type protocol; our in-domain
    numbers should be read as standard-protocol results, and the zero-shot
    VeRi-Wild transfer of Section~\ref{sec:veriwild} is our only probe of
    generalisation beyond the training distribution.
  \item The exact $P \times K$ identity-sampling tuple used to train the
    released Stream~1 checkpoint was not logged
    (Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility}), so from-scratch retraining cannot be
    specified exactly from our records; retraining reproducibility is bounded
    by the $\pm0.55$\,pp seed band of Section~\ref{sec:seed_variance}. A
    controlled comparison of 256\,$\times$\,256 \emph{training} resolution
    against the deployed 224\,$\times$\,224 was likewise not run and would
    require retraining.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% =============================================================================

This paper revisited the ViT-Base/16 pipeline for vehicle re-identification on
VeRi-776 and separated the contribution of the training recipe from that of
evaluation-time post-processing. A fully specified recipe---combining CLIP
pretraining initialisation, a tuned metric-learning objective, an optimised
augmentation pipeline, AdamW optimisation, and a three-step post-processing
pipeline---achieves 89.97\% mAP and 97.79\% Rank-1 at its best-mAP operating
point, with a separate best-Rank-1 operating point of 85.14\% mAP and 98.33\%
Rank-1. As emphasised throughout, this number is delivered by the
post-processing pipeline rather than by the recipe: the recipe merely matches
TransReID at the raw-embedding level (82.2\% vs.\ 82.3\%), and given our
identical post-processing the official TransReID and CLIP-ReID checkpoints
reach 91.12\% and 91.80\% mAP---both above ours
(Section~\ref{sec:matched}). We therefore present Stream~1 as a reproducible,
fully specified baseline rather than a superior recipe---a result we regard as
the paper's most useful finding for the community, since it suggests that a
substantial part of apparent post-TransReID progress on this benchmark is
attributable to post-processing rather than to architectural or recipe
refinement.

Score-level fusion with a CLIP-SENet branch~\cite{lu2025clipsenet} achieves
93.32\% mAP and 98.45\% Rank-1, marginally the best published mAP on VeRi-776,
exceeding the previous best (CLIP-SENet standalone:
92.9\%~\cite{lu2025clipsenet}) by a narrow $+0.42$\,pp; Rank-1 accuracy
(98.45\%) is competitive with but does not exceed the CLIP-SENet standalone
Rank-1 (98.7\%). A paired bootstrap confirms the gain over the stronger single
stream is statistically significant, so the fusion demonstrates genuine feature
complementarity between transformer and CNN-semantic streams. The comprehensive
ablation from 82.3\% to 93.3\% mAP traces this trajectory under one consistent
protocol, and the zero-shot VeRi-Wild study shows which parts of it transfer:
the ViT-B embedding generalises in part, whereas the fusion advantage and the
re-ranking gain are domain-specific.

Future directions include knowledge distillation from the fusion model into a
single-stream model; learned query-adaptive fusion toward the measured oracle
headroom (Section~\ref{sec:veriwild}); evaluation under seen/unseen
vehicle-type protocols rather than standard random-identity splits; and
extension to nighttime surveillance and CityFlowV2. All code and checkpoints
are publicly released.
